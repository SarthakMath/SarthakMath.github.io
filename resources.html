Resources

Free material I find useful for studying mathematics and numerical PDEs: things to play with, things to read, code to run and things to watch.

Interactive mathematics

  • VisualPDEHeat, wave, reaction–diffusion and pattern-forming equations solved live in the browser. You can paint initial conditions and change the equations.
  • ChebfunComputing with functions to machine precision in MATLAB, using Chebyshev polynomials. Its gallery of examples is a short course in approximation, ODEs and rootfinding.
  • Approximation Theory and Approximation PracticeTrefethen’s book on polynomial approximation. Every chapter is a Chebfun script you can run, and the scripts are free to download.
  • Riemann Problems and Jupyter SolutionsKetcheson, Mandli and LeVeque on shocks and rarefactions in conservation laws, as interactive notebooks.
  • Immersive Linear AlgebraA complete linear algebra textbook in which every figure can be rotated and dragged.

Free books and lecture notes

  • Fundamentals of Numerical ComputationDriscoll and Braun. A first course in numerical analysis with code on every page, in Julia, Python and MATLAB. The further reading for Converge.
  • An Introduction to the Conjugate Gradient Method Without the Agonizing PainShewchuk’s classic. The clearest account of why CG works, with pictures of every step.
  • Finite Difference and Spectral Methods for Ordinary and Partial Differential EquationsTrefethen’s unpublished 1996 text: stability, accuracy and dispersion of difference schemes.
  • Solving PDEs in Python: The FEniCS Tutorial ILangtangen and Logg. A hands-on start with finite element software, open access.
  • Applied AnalysisHunter and Nachtergaele. Metric and Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces, Fourier series, distributions and Sobolev spaces, aimed at applications.
  • Notes on Partial Differential EquationsHunter’s graduate notes: Sobolev spaces, elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic equations. A free companion to Evans.

Courses

  • MIT 18.06 Linear AlgebraGilbert Strang’s lectures, with notes, problem sets and exams.
  • MIT 18.085 Computational Science and Engineering IStrang on the matrices behind differential equations: stiffness matrices, Laplacians and least squares.
  • MIT 18.086 Mathematical Methods for Engineers IIStrang’s sequel: difference methods, conservation laws, iterative solvers and multigrid.
  • MIT 18.335 Introduction to Numerical MethodsSteven Johnson’s graduate course: numerical linear algebra, Krylov methods and floating point.
  • MIT Computational ThinkingApplied mathematics taught in Julia with Pluto notebooks, including climate models and PDEs.

Julia for PDEs

Getting started: the Julia manual, Modern Julia Workflows for setting up projects, testing and performance, and Pluto.jl for reactive notebooks.

  • Ferrite.jlA finite element toolbox that leaves the assembly loop in your hands. Good for learning, and for methods that need control over every step.
  • Gridap.jlWeak forms written almost as on paper, from Poisson to Navier–Stokes. The tutorials are a course in themselves.
  • ExtendableFEM.jlFinite elements from WIAS Berlin, including divergence-free and pressure-robust discretisations.
  • VoronoiFVM.jlFinite volumes for nonlinear convection–diffusion–reaction systems, also from WIAS.
  • Trixi.jlHigh-order discontinuous Galerkin methods for conservation laws, with shock capturing.
  • DifferentialEquations.jlTime integration for ODEs, including the large stiff systems that come from discretising PDEs.
  • MethodOfLines.jlTurns a symbolically written PDE into a finite difference system of ODEs automatically.
  • ApproxFun.jlJulia’s answer to Chebfun: functions and spectral methods for ODEs and PDEs.

Linear solvers and preconditioners:

  • LinearSolve.jlOne interface to direct and iterative solvers, so you can swap methods without rewriting code.
  • Krylov.jlCG, MINRES, GMRES, BiCGStab and many more, all accepting preconditioners.
  • AlgebraicMultigrid.jlRuge–Stüben and smoothed aggregation multigrid, usable as a preconditioner for CG.
  • IncompleteLU.jlIncomplete LU factorisation with thresholding, for nonsymmetric problems such as convection–diffusion.

Meshes, output and pictures:

  • GmshThe standard free mesh generator, scriptable from Julia through Gmsh.jl.
  • WriteVTK.jl and ParaViewWrite solutions as VTK files and explore them in 3D.
  • Makie.jlPublication-quality and interactive plots, including surfaces, meshes and animations.

Videos and reading for fun

  • 3Blue1BrownGrant Sanderson’s animated explanations. Start with Essence of Linear Algebra, Essence of Calculus and the differential equations series, which derives the heat equation and Fourier series.
  • NumberphileMathematicians explaining one idea each, usually with brown paper and a marker.
  • MathologerLonger, careful videos on proofs and surprising results.
  • Stand-up MathsMatt Parker on recreational mathematics, and on what goes wrong when people compute.
  • Steve BruntonLecture series on differential equations, Fourier analysis, the SVD and data-driven modelling.
  • Quanta MagazineWell-written news about current research in mathematics.
  • ManimThe animation library behind 3Blue1Brown, maintained by its community, for making videos of your own.
